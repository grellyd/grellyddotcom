What are the essential components of an apology? In my view: stating you are sorry, taking responsibility, showing empathy, and fixing the root cause.

With no reference to a real situation, I will use an imagined scenario where a client of a vet clinic was mis-triaged and their pet did poorly waiting.

1. **State that you are sorry**. Don’t dance around it. Be blunt. Apologize. “I apologize.”
2. **Take responsibility**. Be specific with what you are apologizing for. Demonstrate you understand and agree that an error was made. “I acknowledge our failure to attend promptly to your cat caused their condition to worsen. Our triage protocols failed to indicate the severity of your cat’s condition.”
3. **Show empathy**. Demonstrate you understand how your action impacted the person you are apologizing to. “Coming to our clinic for help and then being forced to wait must have been immensely stressful and hard to bear. You showed immense grace acting as calmly as you did”
4. **Fix the root cause**. Determine how this error can be avoided in the future and state corrective actions you are or will take. How are you going to manage the situation the next time it occurs? How will others be protected from your mistakes? “Due to your experience and complaint, we are updating our triage protocols and retraining our staff to ensure cases like yours are correctly triaged in the future so that this does not happen again."

You can then reiterate some elements and conclude. “I apologize again on behalf of our clinic. We are glad to hear your fluffy friend is doing better. We hope you will consider allowing us to serve you again in the future.”

An effective apology can be practically magic for the giver and the receiver.

After that, it is up to the receiver to choose to accept your apology or not. Acceptance is outside your control. With a good faith genuine apology, I believe the chances of an apology being accepted are higher. 

Should the apology be rejected, proceed with a clear head and open heart knowing you have done what you can. Follow up with your corrective actions regardless of their response; those are for you as much as they are for them.

Should the apology be accepted, the matter is concluded. Follow through on the corrective actions. 

Good luck!

P.S. If this is a situation where apologising may open you up to litigation, talk to a lawyer first. While it has been shown that a genuine apology [reduces the severity of litigation in the case of medical malpractice](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1740-1461.2006.00072.x), the accuracy of that belief has more recently been [called into question](https://review.law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2019/02/McMichael-71-Stan.-L.-Rev.-341-2019.pdf). Talk to your lawyer!
